Any AI can reword your prompt. None of them ask what you actually meant.

So they guess. You type "help me write a song", the AI picks one reading out of three, and you spend the next four messages steering it back to the thing you had in your head the whole time. Your prompt was never the problem. The guess was.

Deepclario asks first.

You type:
  help me write a song

It asks:
  What kind of song?
  [Love song] [Sad song] [Something fun]

You tap "Sad song" and get:
  Write the lyrics for a sad song about losing touch with a close friend.
  Three verses and a chorus. Keep the language plain and specific. Avoid
  clichés about rain and broken hearts. Let the last verse turn toward
  acceptance rather than anger.

One question. One rewrite. No back and forth.

"BUT I CAN JUST ASK CHATGPT TO IMPROVE MY PROMPT"

You can, and it will hand you something longer that is still built on a guess, because it has no way to know which of the three songs you meant. It is not allowed to stop and ask. That is the whole difference: we ask the one question that changes the answer, before anything gets written.

You also have to leave what you were doing, open a second chat, paste, copy, and come back. This happens in the box you are already typing in, in about a second.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

• It asks before it rewrites. "Help me write a song" could mean a love song, a sad song, or something fun. Instead of guessing, it asks you one short question and gives you answers to tap. Then it rewrites, once, for what you actually meant.

• When your prompt is already clear, it tells you so and changes nothing. A tool that finds a problem every single time is not reading your prompt, it is running a template.

• It knows the job. A coding prompt gets what an engineer would ask for: the code, the error, what "working" looks like. A cold email gets what a good copywriter would: a subject line, one reader, one clear ask. It does not use the same shape for everything.

• It remembers your answers. Tell it once that you write for engineering leads and it stops asking. Next time it offers your usual answer first. It offers, it never assumes, and you can make it forget anything at any time.

• Your words are never lost. Press Esc to undo, or open Compare to see your original beside the new one and take yours back with one click.

HOW TO USE IT

1. Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
2. Type your rough prompt in the chat box.
3. Press Alt+I (Option+I on a Mac), or click the small ✦ Improve chip by the input.
4. Press Enter to send, or Esc to undo.

Nothing runs on its own. Your prompt is only sent to Deepclario when you press Improve.
